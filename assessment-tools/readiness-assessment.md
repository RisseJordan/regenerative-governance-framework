## Scoring guide

Use this to interpret your responses independently. If you're completing this before a call with Charisse Jordan, she will interpret your responses directly in your conversation.

---

### How to read your results

Count your responses in each category below. The category with the most responses indicates your primary governance priority.

---

**Category A — Foundation gaps**
*Questions 7, 8, 9*

If you selected "No," "Not sure," or "Nothing formal" for two or more of these questions, your organization lacks the basic infrastructure of AI governance. The starting point is an AI use inventory and a written policy — without these, everything else is operating on assumption.

**Priority:** Restore tier — establish the baseline before anything else.

---

**Category B — Regulatory exposure**
*Questions 1, 6, 10, 12*

If you are in a regulated sector, using AI with sensitive data, and have not reviewed your regulatory obligations — you have exposure you may not be aware of. Regulatory requirements for AI in healthcare, financial services, legal, and education are specific, active, and enforced.

**Priority:** Review your sector-specific regulatory obligations. Start with the policy template for your sector in this repository.

---

**Category C — Operational risk**
*Questions 4, 5, 11, 13*

If staff are using AI tools broadly, you are uncertain what's in use, and there is no process for flagging concerns — the risk is in your day-to-day operations. This is the most common starting point for small and midsize organizations.

**Priority:** AI use inventory first. Then policy. Then staff guidance.

---

**Category D — Vendor risk**
*Questions 9, 11 (vendor tools)*

If AI tools are provided by vendors and you have not reviewed data protection agreements or vendor practices — your exposure may be coming from outside your own walls. Vendor-provided AI tools are subject to the same regulatory requirements as tools you build or adopt yourself.

**Priority:** Vendor vetting and data protection agreements.

---

### Overall risk signal

**3 or more "No" or "Not sure" responses across Sections 3 and 4:**
Your organization has meaningful governance gaps. The risk is real and worth addressing now — not because of theoretical future audits, but because the tools are already in use.

**1–2 "No" or "Not sure" responses:**
You have a partial foundation. Targeted work in the gap areas will significantly improve your posture without requiring a comprehensive overhaul.

**Mostly affirmative responses with specific concerns in Section 5:**
Your foundation is reasonable. The open questions in Section 5 are the right place to focus — specific risks, specific tools, or specific regulatory questions that need targeted attention.
