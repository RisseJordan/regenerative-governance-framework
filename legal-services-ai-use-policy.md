## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how `[ORGANIZATION]` uses artificial intelligence (AI) tools — including generative AI platforms, AI-assisted legal research tools, document review and drafting tools, and AI features embedded in practice management software — in ways that protect client confidentiality, preserve attorney-client privilege, maintain competence standards, and comply with applicable ethics rules.

AI tools are now embedded in the infrastructure of legal practice. Legal research platforms, document management systems, and practice management software all incorporate AI features. A blanket ban on AI is neither realistic nor necessary. What is necessary is a clear, enforceable policy that brings AI use into the open — where it can be supervised, governed, and aligned with the ethical obligations that are fundamental to the practice of law.

This policy is designed to make AI use at `[ORGANIZATION]` ethically sound, professionally defensible, and safe for the clients we serve.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All attorneys, paralegals, staff, contractors, and vendors working with or on behalf of `[ORGANIZATION]`
- All AI tools used in any aspect of legal work — research, drafting, document review, client communication, billing, intake, or administration
- AI tools provided by vendors as well as tools staff access independently
- AI features embedded in existing software platforms (legal research, document management, practice management, email, and office productivity tools)

This policy applies regardless of whether the AI tool was formally adopted by the organization or is being used informally by individual staff members.

## 3. Definitions

**Generative AI (GenAI)** — AI systems that generate text, documents, summaries, or other content in response to prompts. Includes tools like ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, Google Gemini, and AI features in legal research and drafting platforms.

**Confidential client information** — All information relating to the representation of a client, regardless of source, as defined under Model Rule 1.6. This includes information that is not privileged but is nonetheless confidential under the ethics rules.

**Attorney-client privilege** — The legal protection that prevents disclosure of confidential communications between a client and attorney made for the purpose of obtaining legal advice. Privilege can be waived by disclosure to third parties, including AI platforms.

**Hallucination** — When an AI tool generates content that appears accurate but is factually incorrect, including fabricated case citations, statutes, or legal authority. A documented risk across all major AI platforms.

**Shadow AI** — AI tools used by staff without organizational awareness, approval, or oversight. A significant source of confidentiality and ethics risk in legal organizations.

**High-risk AI use** — Any use of an AI tool that involves confidential client information, influences a legal work product delivered to a client, or affects a consequential outcome for a client matter.

## 4. Core ethical obligations and how they apply to AI

The following Model Rules of Professional Conduct apply directly to AI use in legal practice. This section translates each into plain-language requirements for `[ORGANIZATION]`.

### Rule 1.1 — Competence
Lawyers must provide competent representation. Using AI competently means understanding the benefits and risks of the specific tool being used, knowing its limitations, and exercising independent professional judgment over all AI-generated outputs.

**At `[ORGANIZATION]`, this means:**
- No attorney or staff member may use an AI tool for client work without first completing the required AI training for that tool
- All AI-generated legal research, citations, and authority must be independently verified before use in any client work product or court filing
- Attorneys are responsible for the accuracy and quality of all work product, regardless of whether AI assisted in its creation

### Rule 1.4 — Communications
Lawyers must keep clients reasonably informed. Some jurisdictions require informed consent before using AI in client matters, particularly where confidential information will be shared with a third-party AI platform.

**At `[ORGANIZATION]`, this means:**
- `[ORGANIZATION]` will determine and document its approach to client disclosure of AI use, consistent with applicable state bar guidance
- Where informed consent is required by applicable rules, it must be obtained and documented before using AI tools that process client information
- Clients must not be misled about whether they are communicating with a human or an AI system

### Rule 1.5 — Fees
Lawyers must charge reasonable fees. Time savings from AI use must be reflected honestly in billing. AI work product may not be billed at the same rate as equivalent attorney work when the time actually spent was significantly reduced.

**At `[ORGANIZATION]`, this means:**
- Billing practices for AI-assisted work must be reviewed and documented
- Time entries for AI-assisted tasks must accurately reflect the actual time spent
- Clients may not be billed for AI tool costs in addition to attorney time for the same work without disclosure and agreement

### Rule 1.6 — Confidentiality
Lawyers must protect all confidential client information. Inputting client information into a public AI platform may constitute disclosure to a third party and could waive attorney-client privilege.

**At `[ORGANIZATION]`, this means:**
- Confidential client information may only be entered into AI tools that have appropriate data protection agreements in place
- Consumer or free versions of AI platforms (e.g., ChatGPT free tier, Google Gemini personal accounts) must not be used for any client matter
- Staff must understand that removing a client's name from information does not make it safe to enter into a public AI platform — unique fact patterns can re-identify clients even without names

### Rule 5.3 — Supervision of non-lawyer assistance
Lawyers must supervise non-lawyer assistance, including AI tools, to ensure compliance with ethics rules.

**At `[ORGANIZATION]`, this means:**
- Every AI tool used in client work must have a designated supervising attorney responsible for reviewing its outputs
- No AI-generated work product may be delivered to a client, filed with a court, or incorporated into a legal matter without attorney review and approval
- Paralegals, staff, and contractors using AI tools are subject to the same restrictions as attorneys under this policy

## 5. AI tool classification

`[ORGANIZATION]` uses a three-tier classification system for AI tools:

### Green — Standard use (administrative, no client information)
AI tools used for internal administrative tasks that do not involve any client information.

Examples: drafting internal communications, scheduling, general research on non-client topics, marketing content with no client-specific information.

Requirements: No special approval required. Standard data handling practices apply.

### Yellow — Oversight required (client-adjacent, approved platforms only)
AI tools used for legal research, document drafting, document review, or other work that may involve client information — on approved, enterprise-grade platforms with appropriate data protection agreements.

Examples: AI-assisted legal research on approved platforms, first-draft generation for review by supervising attorney, contract review tools with enterprise data protection.

Requirements: Tool must appear on approved vendor list. Supervising attorney must review all outputs. Client information handling must comply with Section 7 of this policy.

### Red — Prohibited (client information on unapproved platforms)
Using any AI tool to process confidential client information on a platform that does not have appropriate data protection agreements in place.

Examples: Entering client details, case facts, or privileged communications into consumer versions of ChatGPT, Gemini, or similar platforms. Uploading client documents to any AI platform not on the approved vendor list.

Requirements: Prohibited. No exceptions. Report any accidental disclosures immediately to `[ROLE]`.

## 6. Confidentiality and privilege protection requirements

### 6.1 Approved platform requirement
Confidential client information may only be processed using AI tools on the approved vendor list. The approved vendor list is maintained by `[ROLE]` and reviewed quarterly.

To be added to the approved vendor list, a vendor must:
- Provide documentation of their data retention and training practices
- Confirm that client data entered into their platform will not be used to train AI models
- Agree to appropriate data protection terms
- Be evaluated for security practices by `[ROLE]`

### 6.2 Privilege risk
Inputting privileged communications into a public AI platform may waive attorney-client privilege. Before using any AI tool with client information, attorneys must:
- Confirm the tool is on the approved vendor list
- Review the tool's terms of service and data handling policies
- Consider whether the specific information being entered is privileged and whether its entry into the tool creates disclosure risk

When in doubt, do not enter the information. Consult `[ROLE]` first.

### 6.3 Data minimization
When using approved AI tools for client work, enter only the minimum information necessary for the task. Do not upload full case files, complete client histories, or comprehensive privileged communications when a more limited input would accomplish the same purpose.

### 6.4 No consumer platform exception
There is no exception for consumer or free AI platforms, regardless of the perceived sensitivity of the information. Consumer AI platforms including free tiers of ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, and similar tools may use inputs to train their models. This is incompatible with confidentiality obligations.

## 7. Accuracy and hallucination prevention

AI hallucination — the generation of fabricated legal citations, case references, statutes, or authority — is a documented risk across all major AI platforms. Courts have sanctioned attorneys for filing briefs containing AI-hallucinated citations.

**`[ORGANIZATION]` requires the following for all AI-generated legal content:**

- Every case citation, statute, regulation, or legal authority generated by an AI tool must be independently verified in a primary legal database (Westlaw, LexisNexis, or equivalent) before use in any work product
- AI-generated summaries of case law or statutory provisions must be verified against the original source
- No AI-generated content may be submitted to a court, regulatory body, or opposing counsel without attorney review and independent verification of all factual and legal claims
- Attorneys are responsible for the accuracy of all filed documents, regardless of AI involvement in their creation

## 8. Billing practices for AI-assisted work

`[ORGANIZATION]` will maintain written billing guidelines for AI-assisted work. At minimum:

- Time entries must reflect actual time spent, not time that would have been spent without AI assistance
- AI tools used in a matter must be documented in the matter file
- Any charges for AI tool costs passed to clients must be disclosed in the engagement agreement or with client consent
- Double billing — charging for AI tool time and full attorney time for the same task — is prohibited

`[ORGANIZATION]`'s billing guidelines for AI-assisted work are maintained by `[ROLE]` and reviewed annually.

## 9. Court and filing requirements

Several courts have adopted or are developing specific rules regarding AI use in filed documents. Before filing any document in any court, attorneys at `[ORGANIZATION]` must:

- Check whether the court has adopted an AI disclosure requirement or standing order
- Comply with any required certification regarding AI use in the preparation of filed documents
- Ensure all citations and legal authority in filed documents have been independently verified

`[ORGANIZATION]` maintains a current list of courts with AI-specific filing requirements. This list is updated by `[ROLE]` as new requirements are adopted.

## 10. Training requirements

All attorneys, paralegals, and staff who use AI tools in their work must complete:

- Initial AI governance and ethics training within 30 days of hire or policy adoption
- Tool-specific training before using any AI tool classified as Yellow under Section 5
- Annual refresher training that addresses regulatory and ethics developments from the prior year

Training completion is documented by `[ROLE]`. Attorneys may count qualifying AI training toward CLE requirements where applicable under state bar rules.

---

## 11. Incident response

If a staff member believes confidential client information has been disclosed to an AI platform without authorization, or if an AI-generated work product containing significant errors has been delivered to a client or filed with a court, the following steps apply:

1. Report immediately to `[ROLE]` — do not wait to assess the severity
2. `[ROLE]` assesses whether the incident triggers notification obligations to the client, state bar, or courts
3. The AI tool involved is reviewed and use is suspended if necessary pending assessment
4. The incident is documented in the incident log
5. Root cause is identified and corrective action is implemented
6. If the incident involves a court filing, `[ROLE]` and supervising attorney assess whether correction or withdrawal is required
