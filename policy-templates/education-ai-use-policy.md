## Important notice on regulatory currency

FERPA was enacted in 1974 and has not been comprehensively updated to address AI, machine learning, or modern edtech. The last regulatory updates to FERPA predate the widespread use of technology in learning environments, including the storage of education records, the technological generation of records, and the use of technology to support and assess students. Organizations must interpret FERPA in light of current AI capabilities and apply its principles thoughtfully to new tools.

This template reflects guidance current as of May 2026, including:

- FERPA (34 CFR Part 99) as currently enforced
- Department of Education enforcement guidance (April 2025 Dear Colleague Letter)
- COPPA Rule updates (finalized January 2025, effective April 22, 2025)
- State-level student privacy laws where applicable (California SOPIPA, and others)
- Colorado SB 26-189 (passed May 9, 2026, awaiting governor's signature — expected effective January 1, 2027)

The Department of Education has doubled down on enforcement and transparency. Schools and state agencies are now expected to demonstrate proactive protections as opposed to reactive response.

Check [studentprivacy.ed.gov](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov) regularly for updates.

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how `[ORGANIZATION]` uses artificial intelligence (AI) tools — including AI-powered learning platforms, adaptive learning software, administrative automation, generative AI tools, and AI features embedded in existing software — in ways that protect student privacy, maintain regulatory compliance, and serve the educational interests of the students and learners in our care.

AI tools offer real benefits for educational organizations. They can personalize learning, reduce administrative burden, support student success, and improve operational efficiency. They also carry significant risk — particularly to student privacy, to equitable outcomes, and to the trust relationships that education depends on — that require active, documented management.

This policy is designed to make AI use at `[ORGANIZATION]` responsible, transparent, and safe for every student and learner we serve.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All staff, faculty, instructors, contractors, volunteers, and vendors of `[ORGANIZATION]`
- All AI tools used in any aspect of our work — instruction, student services, administration, advising, assessment, communications, or operations
- AI tools provided by vendors as well as tools staff access independently
- AI features embedded in existing learning management systems, student information systems, and productivity platforms

This policy applies regardless of whether the AI tool was formally adopted by the organization or is being used informally by individual staff members.

Special provisions in this policy apply to AI tools used with or affecting students under age 18 and, where applicable, under age 13.

## 3. Definitions

**Education record** — As defined under FERPA: records, files, documents, and other materials that contain information directly related to a student and are maintained by an educational agency or institution. Includes grades, transcripts, disciplinary records, attendance records, financial aid records, and any other information maintained about a student.

**Personally Identifiable Information (PII)** — As defined under FERPA: information that can be used to identify a student directly or indirectly. Includes name, address, student ID number, social security number, biometric data, and indirect identifiers that would identify a student with reasonable certainty.

**Directory information** — Information in an education record that would not generally be considered harmful if disclosed (e.g., name, enrollment status, dates of attendance). May be disclosed without consent only after proper notice and opt-out opportunity to students and parents.

**Legitimate educational interest** — The standard under which school officials may access student education records without consent — when necessary to perform their professional responsibilities.

**COPPA** — Children's Online Privacy Protection Act. Requires parental consent before collecting personal information from children under age 13. Applies to commercial operators of websites and online services directed to children.

**High-risk AI use** — Any use of an AI tool that processes student PII, influences decisions about student learning or advancement, or affects a consequential outcome for a student.

## 4. Core principles

`[ORGANIZATION]` is committed to using AI in ways that are:

**Privacy-protective** — Student education records and PII are protected under FERPA and applicable state law. AI tools that process student data must meet the same privacy standards as any other system that handles education records.

**Equitable** — AI tools used in educational contexts must not produce discriminatory outcomes or reinforce bias in ways that disadvantage students based on race, ethnicity, disability, language, gender, or any other protected characteristic.

**Transparent** — Students, parents, and guardians have rights regarding how student data is used. Staff must understand what AI tools are in use and how student data flows through them.

**Age-appropriate** — Different protections apply to students under 18 and students under 13. AI tools must be appropriate for the age of the students they serve, and additional safeguards apply when children are involved.

**Educational** — AI tools in instructional contexts must serve genuine educational purposes. Tools that surveil, profile, or commercialize student data without clear educational benefit are not appropriate for use at `[ORGANIZATION]`.

**Accountable** — Every AI tool used in a student-facing or student-data-touching context has a designated owner at `[ORGANIZATION]` responsible for its appropriate use, vendor oversight, and ongoing review.

## 5. FERPA compliance requirements

### 5.1 Student PII and AI tools
FERPA restricts the disclosure of student PII from education records to third parties without consent. When student PII is entered into an AI tool provided by a third-party vendor, that entry may constitute a disclosure under FERPA.

`[ORGANIZATION]` will treat any AI tool that processes student PII as a third-party service subject to FERPA's requirements. Before any AI tool may process student PII:

- The vendor must be designated as a "school official" under FERPA with a legitimate educational interest, OR
- The vendor must operate under a data protection agreement that limits data use to the educational services being provided
- The vendor must not use student PII to train, improve, or develop AI models beyond what is required to deliver the contracted service

### 5.2 Data protection agreements
Every vendor providing AI tools that process student PII must sign a data protection agreement with `[ORGANIZATION]` before the tool is used. That agreement must:

- Specify what student data the vendor may access and for what purpose
- Prohibit the vendor from using student PII for commercial purposes, advertising, or to build commercial products
- Require the vendor to delete student data when the contract ends or upon request
- Require the vendor to notify `[ORGANIZATION]` of any data breach involving student PII within the timeframes required by applicable law

Vendors who cannot or will not sign appropriate data protection terms may not be used with student data.

### 5.3 No consumer platform exception
Consumer or free versions of AI platforms — including ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot personal, and similar tools — do not meet FERPA requirements for handling student PII. These platforms:

- Are not operating as school officials under FERPA
- May use inputs to train their models
- Do not have data protection agreements with `[ORGANIZATION]`

Staff must not enter any student PII into consumer AI platforms. This applies to individually identifiable student information even if the student's name is removed — other identifying details may still constitute PII under FERPA.

### 5.4 Data minimization
When using approved AI tools that process student data, enter only the minimum student information necessary for the educational purpose. Do not upload full student records, complete disciplinary histories, or comprehensive PII when a more limited data set would accomplish the same purpose.

### 5.5 Student and parent rights
FERPA gives students (and parents of students under 18) the right to:

- Inspect and review their education records
- Request amendment of records they believe are inaccurate
- Have some control over disclosure of their PII

AI tools used at `[ORGANIZATION]` must not impair students' or parents' ability to exercise these rights. If an AI tool generates, modifies, or influences education records, those records remain subject to FERPA inspection and amendment rights.

## 6. Special protections for students under age 18 and under age 13

### 6.1 Students under age 18
AI tools used in instructional or student-services contexts with minors must be:

- Age-appropriate in content and design
- Free from features that collect data beyond what is necessary for the educational purpose
- Reviewed for potential harm to minors before adoption

### 6.2 Students under age 13 — COPPA requirements
COPPA requires verifiable parental consent before collecting personal information from children under 13 through commercial online services. ChatGPT's terms of service prohibit children under 13 from using the service and only permit teens to use it via parental consent.

`[ORGANIZATION]` must ensure that:

- No AI tool directed at or used with students under 13 collects personal information without appropriate parental consent under COPPA
- All AI tools used with students under 13 are vetted for COPPA compliance before adoption
- Staff do not direct students under 13 to use AI tools that are not COPPA-compliant

### 6.3 Students with disabilities — IDEA considerations
AI tools used in special education contexts or with students who have Individualized Education Programs (IEPs) must:

- Not compromise the confidentiality of IEP records, which are education records under FERPA
- Be evaluated for accessibility and appropriateness for students with disabilities
- Not be used to make determinations about special education eligibility or placement without required human review and IEP team involvement

## 7. AI in instruction and assessment

### 7.1 AI-generated content in instruction
Staff may use AI tools to support instructional design, content creation, and lesson planning. AI-generated instructional content must be reviewed by a qualified educator before use with students for accuracy, appropriateness, and alignment with educational goals.

### 7.2 AI in student assessment
AI tools used to assess student work — including automated grading, plagiarism detection, and writing analysis tools — must:

- Be disclosed to students before use
- Not be used as the sole determinant of a student's grade or academic standing without human review
- Be evaluated for bias that could disadvantage students based on race, language, disability, or other characteristics
- Comply with `[ORGANIZATION]`'s academic integrity policies

### 7.3 AI detection tools
Tools that detect AI-generated student work must be used with caution. Current AI detection tools have documented rates of false positives — incorrectly flagging human-written work as AI-generated. No academic integrity action may be taken against a student based solely on an AI detection tool's output without additional investigation and human review.

### 7.4 Student use of AI
`[ORGANIZATION]`'s policies on student use of AI in coursework are governed by `[REFERENCE: ACADEMIC INTEGRITY POLICY]`. This policy governs staff and organizational use of AI. Both policies work together to create a coherent AI governance environment.

## 8. Vendor vetting requirements

Before any AI tool is adopted for use with student data at `[ORGANIZATION]`, the following vetting steps are required:

- **Data practices review:** What data does the tool collect? How is it stored, used, and retained? Does the vendor use student data to train AI models?
- **FERPA/COPPA compliance:** Can the vendor operate as a school official under FERPA? Do they meet COPPA requirements if students under 13 will use the tool?
- **Data protection agreement:** Will the vendor sign appropriate data protection terms?
- **Security review:** What security measures protect student data? Has the vendor had any data breaches?
- **Bias and equity review:** Has the tool been evaluated for potential bias against students based on protected characteristics?
- **Age appropriateness:** Is the tool appropriate for the age of students who will use it?

Vendor vetting is conducted by `[ROLE]` and documented in the approved vendor list. Vetting must be renewed annually for vendors with ongoing access to student data.

---

## 9. Equity and bias requirements

AI tools used in educational contexts carry specific risks of reinforcing or amplifying existing inequities. `[ORGANIZATION]` will:

- Evaluate all AI tools used in student-facing or student-impacting contexts for potential bias before adoption
- Monitor outcomes of AI tools in use for disparate impact on students based on race, ethnicity, language, disability status, or other characteristics
- Require vendors to provide information about how their AI models were trained and what steps were taken to address potential bias in training data
- Not use AI tools to make consequential decisions about student placement, advancement, or discipline without qualified human review

---

## 10. Staff responsibilities

All staff who use AI tools in their work are responsible for:

- Reading and understanding this policy before using any AI tool that involves student data
- Using only AI tools on the approved vendor list when student PII is involved
- Applying data minimization principles — entering only the student information necessary for the task
- Never entering student PII into consumer AI platforms
- Reviewing all AI-generated content involving students before use or delivery
- Reporting any concern about an AI tool's output — including outputs that seem biased, inaccurate, or potentially harmful to students — to `[ROLE]`
- Completing AI governance training as required by `[ORGANIZATION]`

---

## 11. Incident response

If student PII is disclosed to an AI platform without authorization, or if an AI tool produces an output that may have harmed or disadvantaged a student, the following steps apply:

1. Report immediately to `[ROLE/PRIVACY OFFICER]` — do not wait to assess severity
2. `[ROLE/PRIVACY OFFICER]` assesses whether FERPA breach notification obligations are triggered
3. Use of the AI tool in the affected context is suspended pending review
4. The incident is documented in the incident log
5. If students or parents must be notified, `[ROLE]` manages that communication
6. Root cause is identified and corrective action is implemented before the tool is returned to use

## 12. Colorado regulatory note

Colorado SB 26-189 (passed May 9, 2026, awaiting governor's signature) covers automated decision-making technology used in consequential decisions — including education — and requires consumer disclosures, explanation rights, correction rights, and access to human review. For educational organizations in Colorado, this law adds obligations on top of FERPA for any automated tools that make or materially influence decisions about students.

`[ORGANIZATION]` will monitor the governor's signature and any Attorney General rulemaking and update this policy accordingly before the January 1, 2027 effective date.
